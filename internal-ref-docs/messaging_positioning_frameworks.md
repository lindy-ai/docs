# Positioning

## **PLG ICP:** Professionals who live in their inbox \+ meetings

**Persona**   
Professionals who value their time and where work happens in their inbox, calendar, and meetings. They can be a part of a larger organization in senior or customer-facing roles or be founders, entrepreneurs, and solo operators who are trying to scale up business.

**Company size**  
Any size company; but early adopters tend to be VSBs (\<20 employees). 

**Industries**  
Any industry where work runs through email, meetings, and follow-ups. This skews toward consulting, SaaS, professional services, agencies, finance, etc. 

**Constraints**  
High daily volume of email and meetings. Constant follow-ups, scheduling churn, and meeting prep. Little to no admin support. The work is important but repetitive, and it steals time from the work that drives results.

**Motivations**  
They want time back and more control over their day. They will pay for a tool that reliably saves time, keeps them prepared, and helps them stay on top of priorities.

## **SLG ICP:** Businesses where growth is constrained by admin work. 

**Company size**  
Companies with 50-500 employees where a seat-based rollout creates a clear ROI. They adopt new tools quickly and do not require heavy IT lift to get started. 

**Industries**  
Email and meeting-driven businesses with customer-facing teams: Professional services, agencies, consulting, legal, and investment firms.

**Teams**  
Partners, Advisors, Sales, Client Services, Customer Success, Account Management, and Executive Teams. Buyers are functional leaders and operators (or are running an AI initiative for them) who own outcomes and want consistent adoption across the team.

**Constraints**  
These teams are drowning in operational overhead. Coordination, follow-ups, scheduling, and prep create drag on productivity, revenue, and service quality. 

**Motivations**  
They want to adopt AI in practical ways that aren’t a pain in the neck to setup. They will pay for solutions that drive measurable productivity gains per employee and derisk roll-out for a broader group of employees. 

## Core Problem

### What’s Broken

Professionals have become overloaded with work about work. They spend hours every week triaging email, juggling calendars, prepping for meetings, and keeping track of follow-ups. None of this is the work that matters, yet all of it is required to do their job well.  The result is a constant drag on focus, output, and execution.

### Why It Matters Now

Getting work done in today’s operating environment is noisy (constant notifications) and expansive (dozens of tools and surfaces to manage), and employers are taking a magnifying glass to everyone’s productivity. Everything requires context switching and follow up, it’s impossible to keep up, and every hour matters. There’s only so much time in your day, and traditional software doesn’t solve the problem, it just adds to the clutter.

### How our buyers describe their pain 

*"i know AI can do a lot of my manual work, i just dont know how to set it up"*  
*“I am spending too much of my day on work that no one cares about, but if I don’t do it, I look bad.”*  
*“My inbox and calendar dictate my priorities instead of me.”*  
*“I have real shit to do but I spend half my day in my inbox.”*  
*“I spend more time trying to keep up than actually doing work”.* 

## Category

### **Existing Category:** Mix of Humans and Point Solutions

Most professionals solve the “too much admin, not enough time” problem by either not doing anything or if they are really senior, hiring an exec assistant or offshore VA. Some teams (especially customer facing ones) will also use a variety of point tools for things like scheduling and recording meetings (think Calendly, Superhuman, Fireflies, and Otter). 

Either admin staff or professionals end up doing the real coordination and admin (expensive) and tools are mostly helpers around the edges (annoying). It works, but it is costly, inefficient, and hard to manage. 

### **Emerging Category:** AI Assistant

The AI Assistant gives every professional their own executive assistant. It lives in your email, calendar, and Slack, listens across meetings and messages, and turns all of that into actions, follow-ups, and prep.

The market is moving here fast. Analysts expect the broader AI assistant market to grow from roughly $3.5B in 2025 to more than $20B by 2030, driven by adoption among professionals who live in email and meetings.

## Use Cases

### Managing your inbox

**Value**: Start every day with your inbox under control and save time responding to emails.    
**Functionality**: Lindy sorts incoming emails, drafts emails, and prioritizes your inbox for you.   
**Alternative:** Manually going through each email and responding to it or deleting it or using another inbox tool that might make a mistake because it doesn’t understand your style, job, or habits. 

### Prepping you for meetings

**Value**: Join every meeting with perfect context.   
**Functionality**: Lindy sorts incoming emails, drafts emails, and prioritizes your inbox for you.   
**Alternative:** Manually going through each email and responding to it or deleting it or using another inbox tool that might make a mistake because it doesn’t understand your style, job, or habits. 

### Taking meeting notes 

**Value**: Focus more in meetings and never miss details.   
**Functionality**: Lindy records meetings and summarizes them with decisions, action items and due dates. You can also ask questions to your AI assistant about a meeting to find information.   
**Alternative:** Take notes yourself, write summaries, and share them with stakeholders.

### Managing follow-ups 

**Value**: Nothing ever falls through the cracks and work moves faster.   
**Functionality**: Automatically tracks post meeting action items and sends follow-ups to stakeholders.   
**Alternative:** Keeping track of everything in a project management tool or constantly referencing notes to send reminders.
