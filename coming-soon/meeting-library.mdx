Every meeting your team has ever had, in one place you can just ask. No more *"how'd the call go?"* or hunting for a recording. Lindy records, transcribes, and summarizes every meeting you attend, and shared folders make that knowledge available to the whole team.

## How meetings work

When Lindy joins a meeting on Google Meet, Zoom, or Teams, it records the full transcript and writes a summary with action items, decisions, and key quotes. Every finished meeting lands in your personal meeting library at [chat.lindy.ai/meetings](https://chat.lindy.ai/meetings).

You can find any meeting by title, participant, or date range, or just ask Lindy about it.

## Shared meeting folders

A folder is a collection of meetings that a group of people can all see and query. Think **Sales calls**, **Customer feedback**, or **Acme onboarding**. One meeting can live in several folders at once.

### Create a folder

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create the folder">
    Go to the **Meetings** page and click **Create folder**. Give it a name.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set who can see it">
    Choose the visibility: just you, specific people, or your whole team.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Describe what belongs">
    Instead of dragging meetings in by hand, describe which meetings belong in plain language: *"any sales call,"* *"any meeting where a user shares feedback,"* or *"all calls with Acme."* From then on, every finished meeting is filed into the right folder based on its transcript. No rules, no tags, no upkeep.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  When you create a folder, Lindy suggests meetings to add right away, based on your last ~100 meetings matched against your description.
</Tip>

### Share a folder

Once a folder exists, share it with specific teammates or make it team-visible. Anyone you add gets an email notification that you shared the folder with them. They open it and can ask their own questions.

| Visibility | Who sees it |
| --- | --- |
| **Only you** | Just you |
| **Specific people** | Only the teammates you add |
| **Your entire team** | Everyone in your workspace |

### Add or remove meetings

To move a meeting in or out of a folder, open its **⋮** menu and choose **Add or remove from folders**. If you own or attended the meeting, you can also take it out of a shared folder when the folder's policy allows it.

## Chat with a meeting or folder

This is the core interaction.

- **One meeting**: open a meeting and ask about it directly. *"What did we decide?"* *"What were the action items?"* *"What did Sarah say about the timeline?"*
- **A whole folder**: open a folder and ask across every meeting in it. *"What objections came up this week?"* Lindy pulls the answer together from all the calls in the folder and links you back to the meetings it drew from.

## Ask Lindy in Slack

In any public Slack channel, @mention Lindy and ask about meetings. Lindy can answer from:

- **Team-visible meeting folders**
- **Meetings shared with you**
- **Your own personal meetings**, in DMs only

For example: *"@Lindy what did customers say about onboarding last week?"* Lindy searches the relevant folders and answers, with links back to the source meetings.

You can also reference a specific folder by name. Lindy knows which folders you have access to and scopes the answer to just that folder's meetings.

<Note>
  If answering in a public channel would need private meeting context, Lindy moves that part to a DM or tells you it can only answer privately.
</Note>

## Act on what was discussed

From a meeting or folder chat, you can ask Lindy to take action on what came up:

- *"Draft a follow-up email to Sarah based on what we agreed."*
- *"Create a Notion task for the action items."*
- *"Add these next steps to my Notion tracker."*
- *"Send a Slack summary to the team."*

Write actions always ask for confirmation before they run, so nothing happens silently.

### Update Notion from a meeting

If you have Notion connected, say something like *"add the action items from this meeting to my project tracker"* and Lindy creates the page or updates the database entry. It is meant to feel like a natural extension of the meeting, not a separate step.

## Privacy

A meeting belongs to its participants first, not to the workspace admin.

- Your personal meetings stay private unless you share them or opt into a folder's auto-add.
- Workspace admins can configure team-visible sources and rules, but they cannot silently push your private meetings into shared visibility.
- If Lindy needs private context to answer a question in a public channel, it moves to a DM.
